Case Study 01

Data Cafe: Designing the Backstage of AI

AI is only as good as the data you feed it. At EA, I designed a self-serve onboarding system that guides game teams to give their data meaning, so the AI tools built on top of it give answers people can trust.

Service DesignSystem DesignWorkflow AutomationAI Readiness
Role
Sole Experience Designer
Team
1 PM · 2 Data Eng
Timeline
6 Months · Ongoing
Platform
Web · Slack · Jira
The Challenge

Data Cafe is EA's data context platform, the source of truth for discovering, governing and using trusted data across every team and title. As game teams started plugging AI into their work, the AI tools at their disposal kept giving unreliable analysis. The problem wasn't the model. It was messy, undocumented data with no definitions, no metadata and no shared language.

My Approach

The obvious fix was a cleaner onboarding screen. But a team building FC works nothing like a team building Battlefield. So instead of just improving the UX of our data product, I designed the system behind it: a lightweight pipeline that meets teams in the tools they already use and makes adding context part of their normal day, not an extra chore.

01

Garbage In, Garbage Out: Finding the Real Problem

I started by listening. Across 71 user interviews and a survey of 24 people, one pattern kept showing up: demand for Data Cafe was high, but teams weren't ready. Nobody enjoys structuring data or writing context for it. It's tedious, and it takes forever. Meanwhile, every request to build a data catalog or onboard a new team lived in scattered Slack threads, long email chains and a lot of one-on-one handholding.

Data Producers
Data engineers and analysts who own the sources. They know their data best but have no time to document it, and every team catalogs differently.
Need: Publish Without the Busywork
Game Team Analysts & PMs
Consumers on titles like Battlefield, FC and Mobile who need fast, trusted answers, and increasingly, AI agents that can answer for them.
Need: Answers They Can Trust
The Platform Team
A small team handling every onboarding request by hand. Their white-glove support worked, but it couldn't scale past a few teams at a time.
Need: Scale Without Burning Out
"Provide data inventory, glossary/definitions, and searchable metadata so analysts can locate tables and metrics fast… enable users to ask agents, 'where did this metric come from?'"
CRM Engineering · Voice of the Customer

The insight that changed the project: data readiness was the number one bottleneck, and the teams that succeeded were the ones that got guided onboarding. The answer wasn't another interface. It was making that guidance self-serve.

02

The Reframe: Designing the System, Not Just the Screen

I treated workflows and integrations as my design canvas. Each decision moved a manual, invisible step into a guided one that lives where teams already work.

Onboarding started with a Slack DM or an email to whoever you knew on the platform team.
One front door: an onboarding site with a single enrollment form for every team and every need.
Every team got the same conversation, whether they were brand new or ready to publish.
A readiness flow: four paths and two simple yes/no gates route each team to the right next step.
Teams were asked to hand-write descriptions for hundreds of tables and columns.
Automated enrichment: an n8n pipeline pulls context from Confluence, GitHub and Snowflake into Alation.
Requests got lost in threads, and nobody knew who was waiting on what.
Tracked by default: every enrollment becomes a Jira ticket and a Slack alert, with a reply within 2 business days.
03

Meeting Every Team Where They Are

Because every team starts from a different place, I designed a conditional readiness flow instead of a one-size-fits-all checklist. Producers pick the journey that describes where they are today, answer a couple of simple questions and land on exactly one clear next step, whether that's publishing a data product or building an AI agent.

Path A · Fast Path
"I want to build with data that's already in Data Cafe."
  • Browse and request access to existing datasets
  • Create a data product
Path B · Bring Your Own Data
"I have my own data sources to catalog and publish."
  • Gate 1: Is your data cataloged in Alation? If not, enroll for onboarding.
  • Gate 2: Is it enriched with metadata? If not, run the n8n enrichment.
  • Create a data product
Path C · Build an AI Agent
"I want to create an AI agent using existing catalogs or data products."
  • Build, test and evaluate in Agent Studio
  • Publish an agent grounded in trusted Alation context
Path D · Guided
"I'm not sure where to start."
  • Submit one enrollment form
  • The platform team routes you to the right path
One front door for every producer and consumer, built in Figma Make.
Producers choose the journey that matches where they are today.
One signup form for every request, from onboarding to data product creation.
04

Building the Backstage Without an Engineering Cycle

Rather than wait for a roadmap slot, I designed and built the pipeline myself with lightweight tools, then partnered with our data engineers to harden it.

  1. Onboarding Site
    Built in Figma Make
  2. Enrollment Form
    One form for every need
  3. n8n Webhook
    Parses and routes requests
  4. Jira Ticket
    Auto-created and tracked
  5. Slack Alert
    Team is notified instantly
Figma Make: The Front Door
I wrote the site spec page by page and used Figma Make to turn it into a working onboarding site, with separate journeys for producers and consumers, the readiness flow and the enrollment form.
Claude Code: The Glue
I used Claude Code to write the form handlers, webhook payloads and scripts that connect the site to our internal tools, work that would normally have waited on an engineering sprint.
n8n: The Automation Layer
One workflow routes enrollments to Jira. Another enriches catalogs by pulling context from Confluence, GitHub and Snowflake, generating descriptions and pushing them back to Alation, turning days of writing into a 1 to 3 day run.
Jira & Slack: Where Teams Already Live
Instead of asking people to learn a new tool, the system shows up in the places they already check every day. Requests are visible, owned and answered.
The n8n enrollment workflow: a webhook in, a Jira ticket out.
Every request becomes a tracked ticket.
The team is notified where they already work.
05

Outcomes & Reflections

20+Teams Interested Across 4 BUs
10Teams Onboarding
11Data Products Built
41%YoY Usage Growth
71User Interviews
1Team With AI Agents Live
From handholding to self-serve: Onboarding moved out of DMs and email threads into one tracked, repeatable pipeline that the platform team can scale.
Better input, better output: Teams that complete the flow publish data with definitions, ownership and metadata, the context AI tools need to give reliable answers.
Still growing: The work is ongoing, with more titles moving through the pipeline and agents next on the roadmap for Mobile and CRM.

Fix the Input, Not Just the Interface: When AI output is bad, the instinct is to redesign the chat. The real leverage was backstage, in the experience of the people creating the data.

Design Systems and Workflows, Not Just Screens: The most useful thing I designed wasn't a page. It was the path a request takes from a form to a ticket to a person.

Meet People Where They Already Work: Busy teams won't adopt a new tool to document their data. Bringing the process into Jira and Slack made good habits the easy default.

Case Study 02

EYQ – Enterprise Generative AI Platform

I led the design of a governed AI marketplace that gave consultants a secure, compliant way to harness generative AI—moving the firm from uncontrolled tool sprawl to a unified, auditable platform.

Experience StrategyPlatform DesignAI GovernanceB2B Enterprise
Role
UX Lead
Team
1 PM · 6 Eng · 2 Data Scientist
Goal
Build a governed AI marketplace.
Platform
Web Platform
The Challenge

Teams across the firm were quietly adopting external tools like ChatGPT—powerful, but risky. There was no governed way to standardize prompts, share workflows, or even track whether AI outputs were accurate or compliant with our data security policies. We needed to reclaim control.

Our Vision

Rather than build another chatbot, we set out to create a centralized ecosystem. One where consultants could safely use pre-vetted "Skills," developers could publish reusable AI solutions, and admins could maintain complete visibility and compliance. A platform, not just a tool.

01

Mapping the Ecosystem

I started with research into how the firm actually worked. Three distinct personas emerged, and with them came a critical realization: this wasn't just a design problem—it was a platform architecture problem. We needed to understand each group's motivations and pain points deeply, and design flows that would make sense to all three simultaneously.

Client Practitioners
Consulting, Tax, and Assurance professionals needing to augment daily tasks, streamline workflows, and access firm-approved resources without risking data security.
Goal: Streamline Workflows
EY Developers
Technical builders focused on creating and integrating AI solutions via low-code tools. They need clear governance processes for publishing skills and integrating with existing business systems.
Goal: Create Reusable Assets
Platform Admins
Managers responsible for tracking AI performance, monitoring user engagement, handling feedback, and ensuring strict data governance and compliance across global teams.
Goal: Monitor & Govern
02

Experience Strategy & Concepting

We defined core user journeys around selecting an existing skill, creating a custom skill, and monitoring usage.

System Architecture
Designed user flows allowing teams to build skills using contextual data, share with colleagues, and test outputs before publishing.
Low-Fidelity Concepting
Explored core interactions: the AI skill marketplace, prompt library management, and context-aware chat interactions.

Foundations: Wireframes & Systems Thinking

With three personas and competing needs, I mapped out the core workflows early using wireframes. The goal wasn't pixel-perfect design—it was to validate that the platform structure would actually work for all three user groups and that the information architecture made sense.

03

Designing for Adoption: The Practitioner Experience

Client Practitioners—the bulk of our users—needed to feel confident immediately. So we crafted an onboarding experience that made the concept of "Skills" tangible. The welcoming tone mattered. Clear guidance mattered. Because if practitioners didn't understand the value on day one, adoption would stall.

The welcoming onboarding modal introducing the concept of a curated Skills library to users.
The main landing view, allowing users to toggle seamlessly between general 'EYQ Chat' and domain-specific 'Skills Chat'.
Detailed skill profile pages help users understand the specific use-case and accuracy rating before invoking the AI.
04

Core Interaction: Intelligent Guidance in the Chat

The chat wasn't just a blank box. I designed it to be a guided experience—one that respected users' time and offered immediate, actionable suggestions tailored to the skill they'd chosen. The goal: help them get value faster and avoid the paralysis of a blank input field.

The 'Skills Chat' welcome screen provides immediate, actionable prompt templates tailored to the selected domain.
Tooltips guide users on when to use standard AI versus expert, specialized skills.
An active chat session showing clear distinctions between the user's input and the AI assistant's response.
The persistent input area allows users to continue refining their prompts seamlessly.
05

Knowledge Sharing: A Prompt Library That Works

We created a shared prompt library—not just as a nice-to-have, but as a core platform feature. The idea was simple: let consultants tap into the collective intelligence of their colleagues. If someone at the firm crafted a killer prompt, everyone else should benefit immediately.

The Prompt Management dashboard, allowing users to browse pre-tested queries by service line or sector.
06

Empowering Developers: A No-Code Path to Impact

The developer experience was tricky. Developers needed to be able to build and publish AI Skills without wrestling with complex infrastructure. I designed a guided form flow that made skill creation feel accessible—turning intricate prompt engineering into intuitive step-by-step workflows. The goal was empowerment, not complexity.

Step 1: Establishing global properties, targeted user groups, and specific service lines for the AI Skill.
Step 2: Connecting the skill to specific host URLs and defining core prompts before publishing.

But I wanted developers to see the impact of their work in real time. So I built out a detailed Skill Analytics dashboard. Real-time accuracy metrics, execution times, and direct user feedback—all visible immediately. This creates a feedback loop where creators can iterate and improve continuously.

The Skill Details view gives creators an immediate snapshot of accuracy and execution metrics.
The Analytics tab provides charts for Discovery Time and Response Time to monitor technical latency.
07

Closing the Loop: Admin Visibility & Governance

Admins couldn't govern what they couldn't see. So I built a comprehensive dashboard that gave them complete visibility into the entire EYQ ecosystem. Adoption rates, top-performing skills, response times—all at a glance. And when something went wrong, they needed to drill in. That's where our feedback interface came in.

The centralized Admin Dashboard tracking total active users, top 5 skills, and global response time metrics.

Admins needed to audit user feedback at scale. We built a dedicated feedback interface that made it easy to filter, review, and act on hundreds of user reviews across different countries. Rather than waiting for escalations to bubble up, admins could now be proactive—deprecating failing skills and promoting high performers.

No visibility into model performance or potential hallucinations in real-world use.
Full Audit Trail. Admins gained the ability to review specific interactions, export data for compliance, and maintain governance across the firm.
Granular review tracking to ensure the AI remains compliant and helpful.
08

Outcomes & Reflections

78%Global Practitioner Adoption
50+AI Skills Created & Shared
5 minNew User Onboarding
Rapid Time-to-Value: Practitioners could start using AI skills within 5 minutes of signup, dramatically reducing adoption friction across the global firm.
Network Effects: Over 50 AI skills were created within the first year, with practitioners freely sharing workflows across service lines and geographies.
Reduced Risk: 78% adoption meant the firm effectively eliminated reliance on ungovened external AI tools, bringing sensitive work back in-house.

Platform Thinking Over Feature Design: The biggest lesson was stepping back from building "features" and thinking like a platform architect. Enterprise adoption hinges on seeing the whole system—how Practitioners, Developers, and Admins all depend on each other.

Balancing Speed With Governance: I had to hold the line on security and compliance without smothering innovation. Some of the best design decisions came from resisting the urge to over-engineer and instead asking: "What's the minimum governance needed?"

Make the Value Visible: Adoption spreads fastest when teams see tangible wins. By making the skill library discoverable and the reuse benefits obvious, practitioners naturally became evangelists. The tool sold itself.

Case Study 03

SaaS Contract Analyzer

I designed an AI-assisted contract audit tool that transformed a 4-week manual grind into a 1-week process—by making machine learning invisible and human judgment central.

AI/ML UXB2B SaaSFintechPrototyping
Role
UX Lead
Team
2 PMs, 2 Data Sci, 6 Devs
Goal
Audit Time: 4wks → 1wk
Platform
Web · Enterprise
The Reality

Our auditors were drowning in contracts—each one 300+ pages—and manually combing through them was sucking weeks of time and thousands in unrealized billable hours every month. The firm had the domain expertise, the historical annotations, and the data. We had everything we needed except the system to leverage it.

The Opportunity

We decided to partner machine learning with human judgment. Not to replace auditors, but to amplify them. If we could train a model on past annotations and flag likely problematic clauses, auditors could focus their expertise on validation and nuance. Our goal: reduce audit time from 4-5 weeks down to 1-2 weeks—and actually make the work less painful.

Visualizing the manual contract analysis process.
01

Discovery: Bringing Everyone to the Table

I facilitated a workshop that got auditors, data scientists, engineers, and product leaders in the same room. The goal wasn't to design in isolation—it was to build shared understanding. What emerged was clarifying: we needed to respect auditors' expertise while introducing ML in a way that felt like an assistant, not a threat.

Virtual design workshop defining problem statements and high-priority functions.
Human-in-the-Loop
Users needed the ability to confidently approve or reject machine-generated recommendations before they were finalized.
Scalable Architecture
The solution needed to be designed so it could eventually scale across different departments for various document types.
Manual Categorization
Even with ML, auditors required the fallback ability to manually add, highlight, and categorize clauses within the documents.
02

From Sketch to Reality: Rapid Iteration

After the workshop, I sketched out workflows based on the most compelling stakeholder ideas. The real test came when we walked auditors through low-fidelity prototypes. That feedback was gold—it showed us where the flow broke down and what we fundamentally misunderstood about their process.

Initial wireframing focusing on the interaction with machine-identified clauses.

Once the team aligned on approach, I moved quickly into high-fidelity using our in-house Motif design system. Two rounds of stakeholder testing later, we were hands-off with developers. I kept syncing weekly though—design problems almost always surface during build.

Engagement page for contract setup.
Contract analysis view displaying annotated text.
03

The ML Reckoning: Handling Imperfection

The model identified financial sections flawlessly—a win. But categorization was messy. Most clauses got lumped into "other," which meant auditors still had to do the hard cognitive work we'd hoped to offload. This was a moment where we had to make a choice: either over-promise the ML capabilities, or be honest and design around the constraint.

We chose honesty. Rather than pretend the model was better than it was, we designed for a human-in-the-loop workflow. Auditors would be gatekeepers, reviewers, and improvers. To build confidence, I designed a power-user beta—six auditors, one month, with dashboards so we could see exactly where the model was failing and then iterate with the data science team.

Internal team dashboard for tracking model performance.
Detailed usage metrics for power users during the beta period.
04

Outcomes & Reflections

4w→1wAudit Time Reduced
92%ML Model Accuracy
80%Auditor Adoption
Achieved an 80% ML model accuracy in the first month, which soared to 92% after just four months of company-wide launch.
Adopted by 80% of financial auditors across EY, accounting for roughly 50% of the revenue generated by the auditing department.

Bring Engineers Early: The best design decisions happened when I stopped designing in isolation and started designing with the engineering and ML teams. Feasibility constraints aren't limits—they're inputs that shape smarter solutions.

Scope Discipline Is Leadership: I watched scope creep threaten the project multiple times. Pushing back on "nice-to-haves" and keeping the team focused on the core problem was some of the most important work I did.

Honesty Around AI Beats Hype: The moment we stopped overselling the ML and started designing around its actual capabilities, adoption took off. Users respected the honesty. They felt like partners, not guinea pigs.

Case Study 04

Listing Doctor

I led the design of an error-tracking system that helped Spreetail's quality team escape from spreadsheet hell and take control of their listing health—lifting active listings from 82% to 95% in three months.

UX ResearchPrototypingWorkshop FacilitationUsability TestingE-commerce
Role
UX Lead
Team
1 PM, 1 Tech Lead, 5 Devs
Goal
82% → 95% Active Listings
Platform
Web App · SaaS
The Bottleneck

Hundreds of products were broken across multiple marketplaces, and every day without fixes was costing Spreetail millions. But here's the thing: the fixes themselves weren't the hard part. The hard part was finding the errors in the first place. Excel dumps from various marketplace APIs arrived in different formats, making it impossible to prioritize or even see the full picture of the damage.

Our Approach

We decided to build visibility and ownership into the system. Not just aggregate errors, but make their financial impact obvious and give the quality team the ability to claim, track, and resolve work at their own pace. Our target: move from 82% active listings to 95% within the fiscal year.

The tedious task of combing through mismatched error formats from various APIs.
01

Understanding the Work: Workshops That Unlock

I kicked things off with a hands-on workshop—not just asking what people needed, but getting them to actually sketch out their ideal day, map their workflows, and vote on solutions together. Marketplace Managers, Operations, Vendors, and the Quality Team all brought different perspectives. The synthesis was enlightening.

Affinity mapping and identifying high-priority problems.
Unified Overview Needed
Users needed a clean, aggregated overview of listing errors for each individual marketplace, rather than raw excel exports.
Financial Visibility
There was a strong desire to view the direct financial impact of listings with unresolved errors to help prioritize the workload.
Claiming & Noting
The quality team needed the ability to "claim" specific listing errors for resolution and attach notes for ongoing follow-ups.
02

Fast Iteration: Testing Early, Pivoting Often

With insights fresh, I partnered with the PM to map user journeys and convert everything into low-fidelity wireframes. Then we tested immediately. Those walkthroughs with real users gave us permission to iterate fast—and we needed to. The initial mental model was close, but not quite right.

Low-fidelity wireframe establishing the core layout.
Wireflow demonstrating the error categorization process.

We ran another round of testing with these changes. The response was immediate. Users felt understood. They felt like the tool was built for them, not against them. Three months after launch to the full team, we crushed our targets.

High-fidelity dashboard showcasing error metrics.
03

The Beta Surprise: When Users Teach You Why You're Wrong

We launched to eight beta users feeling confident. They loved the interface. Clear error messaging, simple workflow. And then they asked for something that made me rethink the entire approach: they wanted to download error lists into Excel. At first, I resisted. But I listened instead, and here's what I learned: their existing mental model was deeply rooted in the tools they already used. Fighting that was futile.

Revised layout incorporating search by item number.
Detailed error resolution panel.
Financial impact view for prioritization.
04

Outcomes & Reflections

$1.8MInventory Value Saved
90%Active Listings on Marketplaces
+8%Since Launch

After another series of user testing, Listing Doctor was successfully launched to the entire user base! Within the first 3 months, the listing quality team saw an increase in their productivity, allowing them to meet their quarterly metric goals of resolved listing errors.

Reduce Friction Against Existing Mental Models: I learned the hard way that fighting entrenched workflows is usually a losing battle. Instead, I embraced item-number search and export capabilities—minor additions that created massive adoption.

Make the Stakes Visible: The turning point came when we added financial impact to every error. Suddenly, the work wasn't abstract.—people could see exactly how much their effort mattered. Motivation shifted entirely.

Sometimes the Best Users Are Teachers: Beta users told us we were wrong, but in a way that helped us build something better. Listening without defensiveness is a superpower in product design.
